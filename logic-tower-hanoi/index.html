🎯 Objective

Transfer the entire stack of graduated discs from the first peg to the third peg without ever placing a larger disc on a smaller one.

📋 How to Play & Scoring

Click a peg to pick up its top disk, then click a destination peg. You may only move one disk at a time, and no disk may ever sit atop a smaller disk. Complete in minimum 7 moves for 3 disks.

Scored in moves (lower values indicate better performance).

🧠 What This Game Measures

This mathematical puzzle measures recursive problem-solving and hierarchical state planning. It tests your ability to decompose a large objective into nested sub-goals.

💡 Strategy & Gameplay Tips

  • 1. For an odd number of discs, make your very first move directly to the destination peg.
  • 2. For an even number of discs, make your very first move to the middle auxiliary peg.
  • 3. Alternate between moving the smallest disc and the only other legally movable disc.
  • 4. The smallest disc should always circulate in one consistent direction across the three pegs.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the minimum number of moves to solve Tower of Hanoi?

The theoretical minimum is 2^n - 1 moves, where n is the number of discs (e.g. 7 moves for 3 discs, 31 moves for 5 discs).

Can you place a larger disc on top of a smaller disc?

No, placing a larger disc onto a smaller one is an illegal move and rejected by the game engine.

How many discs can be selected in this version?

You can choose between 3 discs (beginner), 4 discs (intermediate), and 5 or 6 discs (advanced challenge).
