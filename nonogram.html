🎯 Objective

Use row and column numerical run clues to deduce which cells to fill and which to cross out, revealing a pixel illustration.

📋 How to Play & Scoring

The numbers along rows and columns indicate groups of consecutive filled squares. Left-click or tap to fill a square; right-click or toggle cross mode to mark empty cells.

Scored in pts (higher scores indicate better performance).

🧠 What This Game Measures

This Japanese picture puzzle measures cross-referencing logic and binary grid deduction. It evaluates how you intersect row and column line run constraints.

💡 Strategy & Gameplay Tips

  • 1. Begin by solving rows or columns where the clue sum plus intervening spaces equals the full grid length.
  • 2. Use overlap logic: when a single run is greater than half the line length, the middle squares must always be filled.
  • 3. Always mark guaranteed empty cells with an X to prevent accidental fills and narrow down remaining options.
  • 4. Cross-check row markings against column clues immediately after filling any square.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What does a clue of "3 2" mean in a Nonogram puzzle?

It indicates a run of 3 consecutive filled cells, followed by at least one empty space, followed by a run of 2 filled cells.

Can you make mistakes without losing the game?

Mistake checking mode gives you up to three penalty strikes for incorrectly filled squares before round end.

What sizes of picture grids are available?

Puzzles start with beginner 5x5 grids and expand up to detailed 15x15 pixel art pictures.
