🎯 Objective

Judge which of two central circles is physically larger despite distracting surrounding circle rings.

📋 How to Play & Scoring

Optical illusions make circles surrounded by large rings appear smaller, and vice-versa. Drag the slider until both center circles are identical in true diameter, then press Submit.

Scored in % (higher scores indicate better performance).

🧠 What This Game Measures

This test measures susceptibility to contextual visual size illusions (Ebbinghaus and Ponzo effects). It evaluates your ability to perceive true dimensions independent of surrounding relative scale cues.

💡 Strategy & Gameplay Tips

  • 1. Block out the surrounding outer circles mentally and compare only the inner boundaries.
  • 2. Measure the inner circle diameter against a stationary finger or cursor edge if allowed in practice mode.
  • 3. Remember that large surrounding circles make an inner disc appear artificially smaller than it truly is.
  • 4. Trust measured geometry rather than immediate contextual impressions.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What causes the Ebbinghaus size illusion?

The human visual system naturally estimates object size relative to surrounding visual context rather than in absolute terms.

Can the two central circles ever be exactly identical in size?

Yes, select trials feature identical circles to test whether you can recognize parity despite unequal surrounding rings.

How does the game score optical illusion resistance?

Scores are calculated from your error percentage across 10 progressive illusion pairs with decreasing size disparities.
