🎯 Objective

Remove matchsticks from any single heap on your turn to force your opponent into taking the final matchstick.

📋 How to Play & Scoring

On your turn, remove 1, 2, or 3 matchsticks from the heap. In this "misère" variation, whoever takes the very last match loses the game. Play strategically to force a win.

🧠 What This Game Measures

This mathematical game measures binary digital sums (nim-sum XOR calculation) and end-game state control. It benchmarks your application of combinatorial game theory principles.

💡 Strategy & Gameplay Tips

  • 1. Calculate the binary XOR sum of heap sizes; an even nim-sum (zero) represents a winning balanced position.
  • 2. Always leave your opponent in a state with an XOR sum of zero at the end of your turn.
  • 3. In the endgame with only isolated single matches left, leave an odd number of heaps containing one match.
  • 4. Force your opponent into 1-1, 2-2, or 1-2-3 symmetrical positions.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between normal play and misère play in Nim?

In this misère version, the player forced to pick up the very last matchstick loses the game.

Can you take matchsticks from multiple heaps in one turn?

No, standard Nim rules state you may take as many matches as you wish, but only from one single heap per turn.

How many heaps are used in Game of Nim?

Standard matches feature three heaps containing 3, 5, and 7 matchsticks respectively.
